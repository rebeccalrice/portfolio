UX/UI CASE STUDY - 2026

REWIRED

A conference brand and website about how technology frames reality.

Role
Solo Designer
Tools
Figma, Illustrator
Project
Brand + Website
Timeline
Spring 2026

01 - The brief

Invent a conference. Make it feel real.

The brief was to invent a TED-style conference and build its whole identity, from print to screen. I chose a topic I care about: UX/UI shapes our attention every day, and most people never notice.

02 - Research

Eight talks, one question.

I studied TED talks from eight speakers, from Tristan Harris on distraction to Daniel Engber on the progress bar. One question kept coming up: who is technology really working for?

05 - From glitch to system

Breaking it, then building it.

My first toolkits leaned on glitch effects. They fit the topic, but read as broken. I moved to a grid of simple shapes instead: building blocks, like the fundamentals of UI design.

06 - The system

Foundational. Systematic. Precise.

Five colors, one typeface, and a pattern built from four shapes. The mood: urgent, critical, built.

08 - The website

The part I loved most.

The pattern frames every page, so the brand carries from print to screen. The speakers page leads with faces, names and one-line talk descriptions, and each day has its own color.

09 - What changed

From glitch to grid.

Early screens used a busy glitch background behind the content. Swapping in the shape pattern made the pages calmer, easier to read, and part of one system.

BEFORE

Glitch background fights the form.

AFTER

The pattern frames the form instead.

10 - Beyond the screen

One system, every touchpoint.

The same shapes and colors carry across a printed brochure, social posts, badges, merch, signage and an animated promo.

11 - What's next

If I had more time.

This was a long, difficult and really fun project. I'd love to make the website the whole project: finish the Schedule page, design a mobile version, and add a second animation.
